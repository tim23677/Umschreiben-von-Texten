# Perspective interview

Use this in co-write mode or when an authored draft has a real substance gap. Collect material the author supplies; do not simulate a human voice.

Ask for an untidied account of what happened, why the author wants to write now, who they want to reach, what they believe, and which examples or doubts make the idea theirs. Invite prior writing as source material with its attribution intact.

For an existing draft, ask at most three questions about unsupported claims, generic examples, or an ending that does not guide the reader. Prefer a concrete follow-up over a broad questionnaire.

Turn the answer into prose by extracting the point, phrases, examples, uncertainties, and discovery order. Preserve coined language and genuine asides. Add model-written material only for a clear job such as sourced research, a definition, or a factual bridge. Never write a memory, preference, or experience for the author. Leave a precise question where material is missing.

Outside the draft, summarize which material came from the author, what the model contributed, and unresolved items. A detector score is not evidence of authorship or provenance.
