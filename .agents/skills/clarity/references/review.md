# Review format

Review the supplied piece. Do not create a replacement draft or change files unless asked. Lead with the largest material issue.

## Piece-level diagnosis

Use relevant fields only: job (medium, reader, outcome), substance, trust (support and attribution), development, voice, ending, and the two or three highest-value fixes.

For reference, procedural, academic, legal, or message-oriented prose, evaluate its actual job rather than demanding an authorial thesis. If substance is too thin for a useful rewrite, say so plainly; surface edits cannot supply missing evidence or experience.

## Passage-level finding

For each material issue, provide:

- **Passage:** shortest useful quote
- **Verdict:** keep / revise / ask-author / cut
- **Pattern:** concise name
- **Why:** what it does instead of its intended job
- **Suggestion:** supported replacement, precise author question, or reason to cut
- **Safety check:** whether the suggestion preserves facts, attribution, scope, and voice

Do not invent missing material. Distinguish errors from improvements and taste. Prefer a few high-impact findings to exhaustive word watching. Do not use a detector verdict as evidence that prose is good, bad, human, or generated.
